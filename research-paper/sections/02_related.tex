\section{Related Work and Research Position}
\label{sec:related}
We compared \method{} with about forty systems and papers; Table~\ref{tab:position} summarizes the closest, and Appendix~\ref{app:related} gives the per-invariant matrix with source or section pointers. Framework code was inspected at pinned revisions.

\paragraph{Agent architectures and frameworks.} ReAct~\cite{yao2023react}, Reflexion~\cite{shinn2023reflexion} and Self-Refine~\cite{madaan2023selfrefine} established the reason--act and self-critique loops that our think--act--critique cycle follows; CoALA~\cite{sumers2024coala} supplies the memory/action/decision vocabulary our layers map onto. AutoGen~\cite{wu2024autogen}, MetaGPT~\cite{hong2024metagpt}, CAMEL~\cite{li2023camel} and multi-agent debate~\cite{du2024debate} motivate our six topologies, and MAST~\cite{cemri2025mast} catalogues their failures. None of these is our contribution. Production frameworks provide the \emph{mechanisms} of pause, resume and approval: LangGraph~\cite{langgraph} persists interrupts and implements cooperative timeouts; Pydantic AI~\cite{pydanticai} wraps approval-required toolsets and declares per-engine durability; CrewAI~\cite{crewai} persists flows but drops callable guardrails on serialization; Microsoft Agent Framework~\cite{msagentframework} matches approval rules to tools and arguments; Deep Agents~\cite{deepagents}, the OpenAI Agents SDK~\cite{openaiagentssdk} and Google ADK~\cite{googleadk} offer subagents, guardrails and plugins. Their own documentation or code shows the boundary problem this paper addresses: subagent budgets and rate buckets are per agent rather than per principal (ADK's agent tool, Microsoft Agent Framework's budget keys); Deep Agents' subagent permissions replace the parent's and default to allow; and approval takes precedence over conflicting sticky decisions in the OpenAI SDK. Their AgentGovBench native rows are \AGBNativeScore{}/\AGBTotal{}.

\paragraph{Delegation and authorization for agents.} Multi-hop attenuation by intersection is established. Bounded Agents~\cite{muruaga2026boundedagents} attenuates scope along a principal chain and adds composition closure, delegation budgets, single-use hash-bound approvals and intent binding, reducing AgentDojo exfiltration to zero at a utility cost of 8.6--13.9 points. Bounded Agents also states cumulative consumption as an invariant---each child inherits its parent's consumed budget as a floor~\cite{muruaga2026boundedagents}---and CapLease charges a delegated child against its parent's authorization budget~\cite{xu2026caplease}; these are our I4 for a single chain or a single authorization, and our addition is keying shared buckets by principal and tenant across sibling threads with exact windows under concurrency (Theorem~\ref{thm:rate}). Further, CAPMAS~\cite{veski2026capmas} and Overlaying Governance~\cite{ibrahim2026overlaying} formalize capability and compositional delegation; Delegation Without Trust~\cite{dantuluri2026delegation} finds LangGraph, CrewAI and AutoGen exposed to confused-deputy and escalation threats and proposes a token broker; MiniScope~\cite{zhu2025miniscope}, SEAgent~\cite{ji2026seagent}, authenticated delegation~\cite{south2025authenticated} and ScopeGate~\cite{zuvic2026scopegate} treat least privilege, mandatory access control, OAuth-style delegation and the gap between exposing a tool and authorizing it. Forbid-first combination is also established: Progent~\cite{shi2025progent} evaluates forbid rules before allow rules with a default block, and ActGov~\cite{zhang2026actgov} and the Agent Governance Toolkit~\cite{msagentgovernance} use deny-overrides. Per-principal budgets across agents exist for risk (Irreversibility Budget~\cite{mohammadi2026irreversibility}), and MasuGate~\cite{peng2026statefulgov} makes budget checks safe under concurrent effects via policy-state serializability. Liu~\cite{liu2026mcpinvariants} names eight security invariants for MCP-style runtimes (principal binding, scoped invocation, data-flow authorization, deny-path audit, among others) and Tallam~\cite{tallam2026fiveplane} four for a governance reference architecture. We therefore claim neither attenuation, deny-overrides, per-principal budgeting nor a named invariant set as new. What we did not find in prior work is the \emph{combination} realized as one execution-scope object in a framework runtime; complete cache identity (I5) as a governance invariant; the non-serialization of live budget and cancellation handles as the persisted half of I3; measurement honesty (I7) stated as an obligation of the runtime's own evaluation layer rather than only of benchmark reporting~\cite{solozobov2026demmbench,zhu2025abc}; a fail mode that a tenant can set to open and that is known during a policy-source outage, with Proposition~\ref{prop:failmode}---existing systems either fail closed unconditionally~\cite{msagentgovernance,muruaga2026boundedagents} or let individual advisory components declare a local error behaviour~\cite{msagentgovernance}; and the benchmark results below. Several designs take the opposite position to I3. CapLease~\cite{xu2026caplease} shows that single-use identifiers cannot bound consumption across retries and crash recovery without durable token-independent state; ResidualAuth~\cite{choi2026residualauth} proves that current reachability can collapse exponentially many future-distinct authorization states under revocation; the Agent Governance Toolkit persists pending approvals across restarts and revalidates the action digest and policy version immediately before execution~\cite{msagentgovernance}; and MasuGate shows that revalidation at resolution is correct but can invalidate a delayed human approval, which durable reservations avoid~\cite{peng2026statefulgov}. In ResidualAuth's terms our re-derivation on resume \emph{externalizes} the query to the live policy source rather than preserving authorization state; it therefore cannot express a spent one-shot approval, and it is sound only while that source is available---the case Proposition~\ref{prop:failmode} addresses. We discuss this trade-off in \S\ref{sec:design}. MasDrift~\cite{xu2026masdrift} measures up to 36.3 points of task-completion loss from strict attenuation across multi-agent architectures; we do not measure this utility cost.

\paragraph{Content and information-flow security.} CaMeL~\cite{debenedetti2025camel} and FIDES~\cite{costa2025fides} track information flow and attach capabilities to data, defending against prompt injection by construction; the design-patterns paper~\cite{beurerkellner2025designpatterns} argues for such architectural defenses. These are strictly stronger than our filter-based I6. Classifier and rail systems---NeMo Guardrails~\cite{rebedea2023nemo}, Guardrails AI~\cite{guardrailsai}, Llama Guard~\cite{inan2023llamaguard}, Prompt Guard~\cite{metapromptguard}, Invariant~\cite{invariantguardrails}---and policy or verifier approaches---AgentSpec~\cite{wang2026agentspec}, AgenTRIM~\cite{betser2026agentrim}, SABER~\cite{cuadron2025saber}, ProbGuard~\cite{wang2025pro2guard}, Conseca~\cite{tsai2025conseca}, ShieldAgent~\cite{chen2025shieldagent}, GuardAgent~\cite{xiang2025guardagent}, DRIFT~\cite{li2025drift}---decide whether content or an action is acceptable. The \gec{} is orthogonal to all of them: it guarantees that whichever policy or detector is used is evaluated with the right principal, tenant, attenuated scope, shared budget and fail behavior at every boundary.

\paragraph{Governance products and audit.} The Agentic Control Plane gateway~\cite{acp2026controlplane} is the closest comparator for our control plane and supplies the strongest published AgentGovBench runs. Microsoft's Agent Governance Toolkit~\cite{msagentgovernance} binds approvals to action digests, policy versions and expiry, consumes them once, and hash-chains its audit log; \method{} has neither action-bound approvals nor tamper-evident audit, and we exclude both from our claims. Its policy engine fails closed on every evaluation error with no fail-open option, and its budgets and rate buckets are keyed per agent or globally rather than per principal; MasuGate's procurement benchmark finds the toolkit's per-agent cost guard exact but its team-budget and inventory decisions stale under concurrency~\cite{peng2026statefulgov}. AgentBound~\cite{kaul2026agentbound}, Auditable Agents~\cite{nian2026auditable} and delegation-ancestry attestation~\cite{liu2026attesting} strengthen audit cryptographically.

\paragraph{Transactions and recovery.} Mnemosyne~\cite{chang2026mnemosyne} admits agent actions as transactions against declared constraints with evidence-preserving repair, and CFRC~\cite{deng2026donotrestart} preserves obligations across stateful handoffs. Both address what happens after admission, which the \gec{} does not.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} Task benchmarks---WorkBench~\cite{styles2024workbench}, $\tau$-bench and $\tau^2$-bench~\cite{yao2025taubench,barres2025tau2bench}, AgentBench~\cite{liu2024agentbench}, GAIA~\cite{mialon2024gaia}---measure capability; AgentDojo~\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo} and InjecAgent~\cite{zhan2024injecagent} measure injection robustness with a model in the loop; AgentGovBench~\cite{agentgovbench} measures the governance layer without one. Kapoor et al.~\cite{kapoor2024agents} argue for baseline-controlled, cost-aware agent evaluation. Trivial baselines are an established sanity check. The Agentic Benchmark Checklist asks authors to report trivial agents and finds that a do-nothing agent passes 38\% of $\tau$-bench airline tasks~\cite{zhu2025abc}; a validity audit of agent-safety benchmarks shows that an always-positive policy outranks five of 21 models on R-Judge~\cite{wang2026validityaudit}; DEMM-Bench reports always-sufficient and always-insufficient anchors for governance-evidence scoring~\cite{solozobov2026demmbench}; ResidualAuth scores counterfactual pairs so that a constant allow or deny policy earns no credit~\cite{choi2026residualauth}; and CapLease checks that legitimate budgets are consumed, ruling out safety by blanket rejection~\cite{xu2026caplease}. Single-component ablation of an enforcement runtime is also established: Liu disables one component at a time on a ten-case benchmark and reports the cases each removal exposes~\cite{liu2026mcpinvariants}. What we add is applying both to a third-party enforcement benchmark and its published leaderboard at scenario granularity---which scenarios a constant runner passes, which controls no scenario observes, and published runs that pass a scenario for a control they declare they do not implement. Per-control ablation is the benchmark analogue of mutation analysis~\cite{jia2011mutation}, and a scenario passed by a constant runner is the analogue of a vacuously satisfied property in model checking~\cite{beer2001vacuity}.
